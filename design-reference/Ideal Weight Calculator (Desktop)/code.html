biotech Clinical & Physiological Context

Formula-based "ideal weight" equations were originally engineered for standardizing physiological drug clearance in clinical pharmacology, not aesthetic or athletic body targets. A person's healthy weight depends on skeletal frame size, lean muscle mass distribution, hydration, and metabolic health rather than a singular formula number.

Mathematical Formulations & Methodological Boundaries

Documented Reference Equations

Height ≥ 60 in

Formulas calibrate baseline lean clearance weight by adding an empirical multiplier for each inch above 5 feet (152.4 cm).

Devine Formula (1974) — Clinical Gold Standard
Men: IBW (kg) = 50.0 + 2.3 × (height in inches - 60)
Women: IBW (kg) = 45.5 + 2.3 × (height in inches - 60)
Robinson Formula (1983)
Men: 52.0 + 1.9 × (inches - 60)
Women: 49.0 + 1.7 × (inches - 60)
Hamwi & Miller Baselines

Hamwi incorporates 48.0 kg (women) / 48.0 kg + 2.7 kg per extra inch (men), while Miller applies 56.2 kg base (men) and 53.1 kg base (women) with smaller fractional height increments.

Clinical Assumptions & Validity Boundaries

science
verified Height Invariance Constraint

Applicable strictly to adults with height ≥ 60 inches (152.4 cm). When calculated for statures below this threshold, historical equations yield mathematically erratic or negative increments because no negative slope was empirically validated.
